## French

### Unit 1: Au café

Usage and register
- [ ] ⚑ `une noisette` (feminine) as the usual form in cafés; `un café noisette` also heard. Is `un noisette` common anywhere?
- [ ] ⚑ `Et avec ceci ?` as the standard “anything else?” in cafés (vs `Et avec ça ?`, `Ce sera tout ?`).
- [ ] ⚑ `Vous avez le wifi ?` vs `Il y a du wifi ?`: which is more natural?
- [ ] ⚑ `C’est libre ?` for “is this table/seat free?” (vs `Cette place est libre ?`).
- [ ] ⚑ `Allez-y` from a waiter inviting you to tap your card.
- [ ] ⚑ A `demi` is “traditionally 25 cl”; `une pinte` 50 cl in most bars.
- [ ] ⚑ `chocolatine` in “much of the south-west”.
- [ ] ⚑ Large milky coffees after lunch as a marker of tourists (custom, stated loosely).

Facts in the background notes
- [ ] ⚑ Prices must include service (“service compris”) since the late 1980s (arrêté of 1987).
- [ ] ⚑ Le Procope opened in the 1680s (usually given as 1686) and is usually named as the oldest Paris café still trading.
- [ ] ⚑ Big Paris brasseries go back to the later nineteenth century; association with Alsace.
- [ ] ⚑ `la carotte` as the name of the red bar-tabac sign; tabacs selling stamps, lottery and transport tickets.
- [ ] ⚑ The croissant’s ancestor (Kipferl) came to Paris from Vienna in the nineteenth century; the 1683 siege stories are legends.
- [ ] ⚑ Price board: invented prices; check they are plausible for a provincial city in 2026 (espresso 1,60 € counter / 2,50 € table).

Pronunciation (IPA in the vocabulary)
- [ ] `[kʁwasɑ̃]`, `[kɔ̃twaʁ]`, `[ɛ̃fyzjɔ̃]`, `[sɛʁvœʁ, sɛʁvøz]`, `[mɔnɛ]`, `[peje]`: standard, but worth a glance.
- [ ] ⚑ `un` [œ̃] “mostly said like in [ɛ̃] in Paris and much of the north”.
- [ ] ⚑ `neuf euros` with [v]: [nœvøʁo].

### Unit 2: Au restaurant (I)

Usage and register
- [ ] ⚑ `la formule` as the usual word for a short (lunch) set menu, distinct from `le menu`.
- [ ] ⚑ `Quelle cuisson ?` as the waiter’s question; answering `saignant` (masculine form) whatever the meat, e.g. for `l’entrecôte` (f.).
- [ ] ⚑ `à point` covering “medium-rare to medium”; French kitchens cooking “a degree less” than British ones (stated as widely reported).
- [ ] ⚑ `à point, plutôt bien cuit` as an understood in-between request.
- [ ] ⚑ `Je peux avoir des frites à la place ?` and `C’est servi avec quoi ?` as natural spoken forms.
- [ ] ⚑ `Tout se passe bien ?` as the waiter’s check during the meal.
- [ ] ⚑ `Pour madame` when ordering for one’s partner: still normal and polite.
- [ ] ⚑ `Un pichet de vingt-cinq ou de cinquante centilitres ?`: pichets usually 25 / 50 cl (sometimes 46 cl “pot lyonnais” in Lyon).
- [ ] ⚑ `Quel vin vous me conseillez ?` (test t3): colloquial word order; acceptable, or prefer `Quel vin me conseillez-vous ?`

Facts in the background notes
- [ ] ⚑ Lunch roughly 12–14 h, dinner from about 19 h 30; kitchens often closed in between; later in the south and in big cities.
- [ ] ⚑ Bread free and placed on the tablecloth; butter the exception in an ordinary restaurant.
- [ ] ⚑ British usage of “entrée” mostly follows the French (starter).
- [ ] ⚑ `café gourmand` as a popular espresso-with-small-desserts option.
- [ ] ⚑ Magret normally served pink; chefs reluctant to cook it further.
- [ ] ⚑ `Entrecôte à la bordelaise` as the Bordeaux speciality.
- [ ] ⚑ Price board: formule 19,50 € / 24,00 €; plausible for a provincial brasserie in 2026?

Pronunciation
- [ ] ⚑ `porc` [pɔʁ] with silent c; `persil` with or without final l.
- [ ] ⚑ `cinq` “increasingly keeps its [k]” before consonants.
- [ ] `[ɑ̃tʁəkot]`, `[kɥisɔ̃]`, `[sɛɲɑ̃]`, `[aɲo]`, `[butɛj]`, `[desɛʁ]`: standard, but worth a glance.

### Unit 3: Au restaurant (II)

Usage and register
- [ ] ⚑ `C’est moi qui invite` / `Je vous invite` for “it’s on me”.
- [ ] ⚑ `Il nous manque une fourchette` as the natural way to say a fork is missing.
- [ ] ⚑ `Vous prenez la carte ?` for “do you take cards?”.
- [ ] ⚑ `les fruits à coque` as the allergen term; `les noix` used loosely for nuts in general.
- [ ] ⚑ `végan`/`végane` vs `végétalien(ne)`: which is more usual in restaurants now? Pronunciation [vegɑ̃] or [vegan].
- [ ] ⚑ `c’est trop bon` as informal praise, mostly among young people.
- [ ] ⚑ `un ticket` for a till receipt; `une facture` for a business invoice.

Facts in the background notes
- [ ] ⚑ Bill never brought unasked, except where there are several sittings or a busy lunch.
- [ ] ⚑ Some restaurants dislike splitting the bill item by item for large groups.
- [ ] ⚑ Tip suggestions now appearing on card terminals in tourist areas and some Paris restaurants.
- [ ] ⚑ EU Regulation 1169/2011: information on 14 allergens for non-prepacked food; in France it must be available in writing.
- [ ] ⚑ Since 2021 restaurants must provide a container for leftovers on request (anti-food-waste law; confirm the law and date).
- [ ] ⚑ Parmesan traditionally made with animal rennet.
- [ ] ⚑ VAT rates: 10 % restaurant food and soft drinks, 20 % alcoholic drinks (as of 2026).
- [ ] ⚑ Canuts: Lyon silk weavers of the Croix-Rousse, revolts in the 1830s (1831, 1834).

Pronunciation
- [ ] ⚑ `je ne sais pas` reduced to [ʃepa] in fast speech.
- [ ] `[kɥijɛʁ]`, `[glytɛn]`, `[puʁbwaʁ]`, `[ɑ̃n‿ɛspɛs]`, `[veʒetaʁjɛn]`: standard, but worth a glance.
